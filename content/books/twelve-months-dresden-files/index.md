## Summary & Notes

The battle of Chicago is won, but Harry Dresden suffered heavy losses. While he's doing his best to pick up the pieces, it's not easy. His boss wants him to marry a vampire, his brother is in critical condition, and there's conflict brewing between the normies and the magical community.

This was a slower burn that other books in the series, but that was by design. The status quo is completely wrecked and the emotional damage from the last book is front and center as we grieve along Harry. While there's plenty of action, the regular formula that these books follow isn't present. Harry isn't doing his detective thing as usual. He's running around putting out fires in the space between dealing with his own pain (and mine.)
