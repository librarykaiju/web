Roland takes the child the Deep Wood, but cannot enter with him. The child screams in protest (the first time he has cried in the story, and Roland is alarmed.) They go to the Lost Home to seek the counsel of his friend Beren.
 
Both Beren and Roland are suspicious of the child, but Beren’s wife scolds them for their cruelty and takes the child to care for it for the night.
 
The two talk about their past as adventurers and where their paths have led them. Beren hung up his sword and opened the Inn. Roland kept on adventuring; helping where he could.
 
On the surface his life looks at peace, like he won a great reward. But he is as haunted by his past deeds as Roland is. And both paths are a penance for their shared betrayal.
