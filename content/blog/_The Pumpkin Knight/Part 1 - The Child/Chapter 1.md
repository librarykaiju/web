The scent of horse filled Sir Roland’s nostrils as Calliope leaned down to nuzzle the knight’s face. It was not yet dawn; a thin ribbon of golden light barely visible in the eastern sky, but the horse was hungry and wanted her breakfast.  
Sir Roland grumbled in displeasure, rolled over, and threw his woolen blanket over his head.  
Calliope chuffed in annoyance. She leaned down and firmly shoved the large lumpen shape under the blanket and was answered with soft nasally snores for her efforts. Taking the edge of the blanket in her mouth, Calliope drug it off of the knight and carried it away from their camp beneath the boughs of a sycamore tree. When she returned to Roland after ensuring the blanket was well out of reach, she found him curled into a tight ball, shivering slightly, but still asleep. The horse stamped her hoof indelicately next to the knight’s head and neighed loudly, but her companion did not stir.  
Finally tiring of this game, she leaned down a nipped hard at the knight’s backside. Roland screamed as he bolted to his feet. He eyed the horse in disbelief. “What in the seven hells was that for?” he screamed.
