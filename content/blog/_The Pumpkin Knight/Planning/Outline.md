_Book One (The Child):_  
**1 - The World is Not What it Seems (Prologue & Chapter 1)**  
**Prologue: Interior of the Deep Wood. Reynard the Fox accomplishes a sinister task for his unseen master. In the distance he sees a figure chopping wood**  
**Chapter 1: Sir Roland Brightsong, a knight errant finds a baby beneath a tree. The baby was born from an enchanted pumpkin seed cast out into the world via Reynard's trickery. The child is essentially a Jack-o-Lantern with a body made of ropy vines. Sir Roland is horrified because magic should not exist in the world. He lifts his boot to smash the head of the baby, but stops short when the child elicits a very human-sounding cry.**  
**2 - Putting the Players in Action**  
(You introduce the important characters and the primary internal conflict)  
**Chapter 2. Roland takes the child to the LOST HOME, an Inn at the edge of the Deep Wood. He has a long history with the proprietor, Beren Spark. He has stopped here on his way to take the child to Queen Bryghid of the Good Folk. Both Roland and Beren treat the baby like a monster; Beren's wife Lillian scolds them both for their wretched behavior and takes the child and cares for it throughout the night*.**  
**Beren and Roland spend the night reflecting on their past adventures and how different their lives are now. Beren hung up his sword and opened the Inn, while Roland kept on adventuring as a free Knight who helps where he can.** **While Beren's life seems peaceful, almost idyllic, it is clear that this life is not one he has chosen so much as it is a duty he feel's responsible for.**
 
**Both men are haunted by a shared experience from their past.**
 
***Lillian and Beren have been trying to have a child for some time, but are unable. Soon after her evening taking care of the baby, she becomes pregnant. Their daughter is the only other person besides Beren, Roland, and the Pumpkin Knight who can travel the Deep Wood in relative safety for reasons to be revealed later.**** #idea
 
**Chapter 3. Roland takes the Child into the Deep Wood to the Good Folk. While there we learn that magic has been deliberately locked away within the borders of the Deep Wood. Queen Bryghid and her subjects are the keepers of the Deep Wood, and the custodians of magic. Through their discussion it is revealed that Bryghid, Roland, and Beren were members of a band of adventurers who earned reknown as the Four Heroes, but that their last adventure ended in some tragedy that left their fourth companion dead. Bryghid and Beren are now bitter enemies, and should Beren ever set foot in the Deep Wood he would be sentenced to death.**
 
**Bryghid cannot give Roland any satisfactory answers as to why the child exists, but can confirm that he is a product of the Deep Wood. She suspects that the child's existence in the wider world might manifest magic in the world again. Roland resolves to kill the child, but is moved by compassion before committing the act. He resolves to raise the child as his own.**
 
****Bryghid and Beren serve a similar purpose: both live to safeguard the Deep Wood from sinister influence, but they are not allies in this and thus are ultimately ineffectual.** #idea
 
**3 - Setting them on the Path**  
**Chapter 4. Flash forward seven years. Sir Roland is visiting a friend from his soldiering days. Sir Balin Thorne serves as the master-at-arms for King Tristan. This is a somewhat comfortable scene. It's clear that the household is used to seeing Sir Roland and his strange charge. The child and the princess are playing in the yard. From their play we can see that the child is sensitive and kind. The men watch them play from afar. It is clear that Balin is still somewhat unsettled by the child, though Roland doesn't seem to notice. Balin takes note of the child's sensitive nature and asks Roland if he is not concerned about whether or not he will be prepared for the "world outside."**
 
**"Sir Balin, how do you feel when you are in battle?"**  
**"On fire. It feels like electricity is coursing through my veins. My heart sings with it. My limbs are strong and light. My eyes are sharp Everything is clear. Simple. I feel alive."**  
**"And now? How do you feel now?"**  
**"Adrift. Without purpose. There's a crawling sensation to my skin that won't allow me to sit still for long. My mind is muddy and made slow by my own thoughts which hammer inside my skull like a sword on a shield. It's maddening."**  
**"It is the same for me. Is it not strange that we fight so hard for peace, yet can't tolerate such a state? Does it not say something about us that we would prefer the blood and thunder of battle to quiet days of birdsong and the laughter of our children?"**  
**"What are you getting at, old friend?"**  
**"The world will teach him how to wage war, my Friend. I want to teach him how to reap the spoils of peace.**
