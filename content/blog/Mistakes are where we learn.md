I came across this Youtube video by Justin Sung titled Thinking on Paper. In the video Justin says that the traditional practice of note-taking teaches us to regurgitate information as perfectly as possible. He distinguishes this practice as wholly separate from -- and antithetical to -- learning which is making connections between information to gain deeper understanding. The video distills his strategy for note-taking into three simple points:
1. Make it wrong 
2. Make it shorter
3. Make it again
The key principle of this strategy is to capture the essence of those concepts that jump out at you as you consume information in as few words as possible, and then to revisit and weigh your understanding of those concepts and refine your notes as you go. The learning comes from engaging with those initial ideas, figuring out what makes sense and what doesn't, and then trying to make new connections. 
This resonated with me because it describes a kind of mental kinesthetics that I engage in before learning a new skill in the Makerspace or when learning to code. I absorb enough information from tutorials or online sources to grasp the basics before diving in to try things out. Through the process of “doing” and applying those ideas I absorbed, I figure out where I made wrong assumptions, refine my technique, and think of new approaches to try.
The relation between my process and Thinking on Paper is that both require engagement and learning happens when we test what we think we know against the material and it requires us to make adjustments. Mistakes are where learning happens. Anything else is just emulation of information.
